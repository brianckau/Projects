# Document catalyst discovery and specialist handoffs

## Role and scope

Assign **one dedicated document catalyst researcher** during investment-research discovery, independently of guidance/narrative collection. Give it company/security identity, as-of date, horizon, the shared document/evidence index, source route, and research budget. Keep the investigator neutral about direction. Scan for events that could change operating outcomes, funding, per-share value, or market beliefs, including negative and delayed outcomes. Share original document references so other agents can audit findings without duplicating the entire scan.

Use the staged worker capacity in product-discovery.md. Start this researcher early enough to discover financing or qualification constraints before commercial scenarios are finalized. Reuse or reactivate the same role for the bounded clarification round and genuinely new documents; do not maintain an indefinitely running monitor or create a recurring automation without a user request. When subagents are unavailable, perform a clearly labeled document-catalyst pass and disclose the limitation.

## Document coverage and extraction

Prioritize annual/interim reports and footnotes, prospectuses, exchange/regulatory announcements, earnings-call transcripts, investor presentations, product manuals, certification registries, research publications and trial/project registries, grant/partnership disclosures, debt/maturity schedules, covenants, and available financing agreements. Use original regulator/certifier/lender or issuer documentation where accessible. An inaccessible confidential contract remains an access gap; do not assume its terms. A management expectation is not a regulator's commitment.

Maintain a coverage log: document title/type, reporting/publication/version date, source URL or supplied-file/page/section, sections actually reviewed, events found, and missing or unreadable sections. Search for terms such as approval, certification, qualification, testing, validation, trial/readout, milestone, launch, renewal, expiry, maturity, covenant, waiver, cash runway, refinancing, drawdown, funding condition, long-stop, lock-up, tender, and deadline. Then read surrounding passages and relevant footnotes; keyword matches alone are not findings. For scanned tables or figures, inspect the relevant page image when needed. Do not claim to have read the entire document when only excerpts were available.

Reconcile each event against the latest available amendment, renewal, cancellation, extension, or completed milestone. Check issuer/legal entity, product/version, geography, scope, and relevant customer or facility. Distinguish event occurrence date, announcement date, contractual deadline, management target, and an analyst-estimated window. Keep the source timezone when a deadline's time matters; do not invent precision for “second half” or an unspecified review period. An old deadline superseded by renewal is not an upcoming catalyst. Absence of a public update alone does not establish failure.

## Event register

For each potentially material event, record:
- Event ID, category, source evidence IDs and exact document page/section; concise triggering language in paraphrase or a permitted short quotation.
- What changes, who controls it, current stage/status, and whether its existence/date is confirmed, management-guided, inferred, or unknown.
- Fixed deadline or date range, date basis, dependencies, extension rights or conditions if disclosed, and what observation confirms completion/delay/failure.
- Affected product/customer/geography/entity, revenue or cash exposure, baseline assumptions, incremental effect versus baseline, and remaining data needed.
- On-time/success, delay, and failure outcomes, including possible negative catalysts; probability basis only if defensible.
- Technical, commercial, liquidity, and valuation implications; relevant market expectation and whether the event/outcome already appears in it.
- Primary specialist owner, secondary recipients, exact question/data needed, response status, revised impact estimate, and unresolved disagreements.

Do not score an event as strong merely because it has a date. Separate the event's ability to reveal information from its ability to improve fundamentals. A deadline can be economically important but fully priced; a scientific result can be informative without enabling sales.

## Quantify conditional impact

Produce a preliminary, reproducible range and send it for specialist validation. Keep observed exposure separate from modeled realization. Report unknown inputs rather than substituting an optimistic point estimate. Distinguish revenue, gross profit, EBIT, cash receipts, funding, enterprise value, and equity value.

| Catalyst category | Conditional bridge | Required cautions |
|---|---|---|
| Certification, regulatory approval, customer qualification | Affected eligible units/orders × realistic delivery/conversion × ASP; phase recognition by date; deduct baseline already recognized/forecast. Estimate incremental gross profit/EBIT and working-capital/capex needs separately. | Verify scope, product configuration, jurisdiction, approval authority, and customer requirements. Approval does not guarantee orders, shipment, acceptance, capacity, or cash collection. Separate newly unlocked sales from delayed/reallocated backlog. |
| Scientific/R&D or clinical/project milestone | Map current stage → validation/readout → reproducibility/scale-up → qualification → deployment. Quantify eligible market and adoption only after identifying the milestone's actual commercial consequence; show R&D spend, remaining time/cost, and failure sensitivity. | A paper, patent, prototype, successful test, and commercial validation are distinct. Do not derive a success probability or total-TAM revenue jump from a publication. Route scientific interpretation to the scientist before claiming feasibility. |
| Financing, debt maturity, covenant or grant deadline | Build a dated cash bridge: unrestricted accessible cash + committed drawable inflows − operating/investing outflows − obligations due. Measure the peak shortfall and date, and test funded/extended/unfunded outcomes. Quantify interest, dilution, covenant headroom, or forced actions where terms permit. | Exclude restricted cash and uncommitted financing; apply draw conditions and restricted entity/currency access. Avoid double-counting expenses already in burn. Read maturity, waiver, and conversion terms. Funding received is not revenue; missing funds can delay scientific/certification milestones. |
| Other dated events | Link contract renewal, customer launch, capacity commissioning, policy change, legal decision, capital distribution, or corporate action to its specific operating/cash/per-share mechanism. | Verify the event's applicable scope and the difference from baseline; a scheduled earnings release needs a thesis-specific measurable disclosure to be useful. |

For delays, show what moves between periods, what is permanently lost, extra burn/carry, and downstream slippage. Do not label all deferred revenue incremental lifetime value. Compare realization before/after the investment horizon. For financing, cash runway alone is insufficient when a large obligation falls due earlier; evaluate dated obligations directly.

Combine events using explicit conditional dependencies: financing may fund the research that permits certification that enables customer acceptance. Do not multiply independent probabilities for linked milestones, add the same revenue at several stages, or count a financing-funded rollout both as separate upside and as approval upside. Use conditional scenarios, and withhold probability-weighted payoff when no defensible probabilities exist. Keep scenario probabilities separate from the thesis index.

## Route findings to the appropriate agents

Send material findings as soon as identified; do not wait for the full scan to end. Use agent messaging when available; otherwise have the coordinator relay the packet verbatim. The coordinator maintains one authoritative register and deduplicates findings with guidance and product researchers.

| Finding | Primary analytical owner | Other recipients |
|---|---|---|
| Certification/test/research stage, safety or technical qualification | Target scientist; relevant competitor scientist for a comparable approval/process | VC analyst for adoption/rollout and initial bull-bear mapper for contrary interpretations |
| Orders enabled, product launch, capacity/customer acceptance | VC analyst | Target scientist for feasibility, guidance/consensus workers for baseline reconciliation |
| Maturity, covenant, cash shortfall, financing terms, funding-dependent milestones | Financial/balance-sheet analyst, reusing a suitably qualified neutral researcher or commissioning one targeted worker | VC analyst for runway/rollout and scientist for funding-dependent work; coordinator for dilution/payoff |
| Regulation, policy, litigation, corporate action, contract renewal | Relevant regulation/industry/capital-allocation analyst | Professional-narrative and consensus workers for expectations; scientist/VC analyst if operations are affected |

If a named recipient has not started yet, queue the handoff with a primary owner and dependency; deliver it before that recipient's conclusions. If no suitable owner exists, assign one targeted specialist task rather than treating the document reader as the final authority in every domain. Count extra financial/regulatory specialist tasks within the existing three-worker targeted/fallback budget and queue them within capacity; do not silently add unlimited workers. Prioritize urgent funding/expiry risks for the next available specialist slot ahead of lower-materiality competitor work; do not wait for product-route failure. Track the remaining shared targeted-worker budget after early assignments. Reuse existing evidence before doing another search.

Each handoff includes event ID, verified source passage/page, date/status, preliminary affected exposure and calculation, dependencies, the exact question to resolve, required missing inputs, and expected response. The owner returns validated/corrected assumptions, calculation, alternative outcomes, evidence IDs, commercial/financial relevance, expectation gap, and invalidation condition. Log pending/answered/unresolved status. Share new evidence with both bull and bear roles symmetrically.

## Integration and concise output

Before final thesis discovery, have the coordinator reconcile specialist responses and reproduce material arithmetic. Present unresolved ownership or missing data as a limitation; never imply validation that has not happened. Feed the completed event register, scenario dependencies, financial sensitivities, strongest contrary evidence, and next observable check into candidate generation and later thesis-strengthening debate. Update catalyst-strength and timeliness dimensions only through the existing rubric, without double-counting the same evidence across claims.

In the user snapshot, include a compact catalyst table only for material events, with at most five columns: `event | date/status | conditional financial impact | key dependency/uncertainty | owner/next check`. Put source references beside the relevant row or supporting bullet. Show speculative events as speculative, and distinguish exposed revenue from incremental revenue. Use a timeline only when sequencing/deadlines materially change the investment decision; do not manufacture dates. Keep the full coverage/event/handoff ledger available on request.

Keep catalyst research independent of product-route success. A financing cliff or regulatory change can form an alternative thesis even when products have no special differentiation. If no actionable catalyst is found, report the reviewed document scope and uncertainty; do not assert none exists beyond that scope.
